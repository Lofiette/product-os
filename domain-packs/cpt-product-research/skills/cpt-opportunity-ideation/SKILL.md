# CPT Opportunity Ideation

## Use when

- The current solution is adequate but likely improvable.
- A new signal, constraint, or hidden expectation may justify alternatives.

## Do not use when

- The task needs evidence collection, not ideas.
- The implementation is blocked by a known defect with an obvious fix.

## Required inputs

- Decision or opportunity, constraints, evidence, approved scope, taste/quality criteria, and cost/risk bounds.

## Method

1. Classify the trigger and decision impact before ideating.
2. Choose one method suited to the problem: focal objects, synectics, SCAMPER, morphological matrix, inversion, pre-mortem, TRIZ-lite, or opportunity-solution tree.
3. Generate divergent candidates without presenting them as evidence.
4. Use creative-tension perspectives: clarity, craft, system, user, business, risk, and challenger.
5. Filter candidates by value, evidence, feasibility, maintainability, risk, and testability.
6. Separate in-scope polish from scope/acceptance/architecture changes.
7. Present at most a small prioritized proposal set and request approval before scope changes.

## Output contract

Produce a compact artifact containing:

- `Trigger and method used.`
- `Candidate set and selection criteria.`
- `Shortlist, rejected ideas, evidence gaps, and test plan.`
- `Proposal classification and approval requirement.`

## Evidence standard

- Creative methods generate hypotheses only.

## Stop and escalate

- Ideation repeatedly reopens an approved decision without new evidence.
- The proposal changes scope without approval.

## Failure modes to avoid

- Running five ideation methods for a trivial task.
- Calling novelty a user benefit.
