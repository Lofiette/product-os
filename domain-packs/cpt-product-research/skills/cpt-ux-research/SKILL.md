# CPT UX Research

## Use when

- A product/design decision depends on user behavior, comprehension, workflow, or usability evidence.

## Do not use when

- The task only requires market/category research.
- The change is obvious and low-risk with no meaningful user uncertainty.

## Required inputs

- Decision, research questions, target users, product stage, available artifact/prototype, constraints, prior evidence, and data-handling requirements.

## Method

1. Choose generative, evaluative, longitudinal, field, diary, survey, analytics, or mixed approach from the decision.
2. Define participant criteria, sample logic, recruiting, consent, incentives, and exclusions.
3. Create neutral protocol, tasks, probes, success criteria, and instrumentation.
4. Separate observed behavior, participant interpretation, researcher inference, and recommendation.
5. Plan note structure, coding, triangulation, outlier handling, and confidence.
6. Identify validity threats: selection, leading questions, prototype fidelity, novelty, social desirability, and small sample.
7. Define decision rules and research repository handoff.

## Output contract

Produce a compact artifact containing:

- `Decision and research-question matrix.`
- `Method/sample/recruiting plan.`
- `Protocol or usability tasks.`
- `Synthesis/confidence/validity plan.`
- `Operational and privacy checklist.`

## Evidence standard

- Quotes alone are not findings.
- Frequency in a small qualitative sample is not population prevalence.

## Stop and escalate

- Target participants or decision are undefined.
- Consent/privacy requirements are unresolved.

## Failure modes to avoid

- Asking users what feature to build.
- Combining opinion and observed behavior into one finding.
